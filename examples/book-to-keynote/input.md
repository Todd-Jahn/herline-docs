# Input

What was provided to Herline to generate this keynote.

## Source Book

**Title**: Thinking, Fast and Slow
**Author**: Daniel Kahneman
**Year**: 2011
**Format**: EPUB (user's own copy, already deep-read once)

## User Bio (as entered in Assessment)

> Product manager with 8 years in consumer tech. Worked on recommendation systems at two unicorns, currently leading a cross-functional team of 12 at a Series B company. Background in cognitive science (undergrad) — rusty but the instincts are there. Read widely in behavioral economics and design thinking. Comfortable speaking to technical and non-technical audiences. Writing style: direct, concrete, allergic to jargon.

## Target Audience

> Company all-hands, roughly 200 people. Mix of product managers, engineers, designers, marketers, and operations. Technical enough to handle nuance but not all familiar with behavioral economics. Attention span: 30 minutes max, will lose them if I go full academic.

## Keynote Goal

> Give the audience three takeaways they can actually use tomorrow when making product decisions. Not "here's a cool book I read." Show the specific ways our default product instincts mislead us, and how to recognize the moments when that happens. End with a concrete commitment I want people to make.

## Format Constraints

- **Duration**: 30 minutes including Q&A (~22 min speaking, 8 min Q&A)
- **Slides**: yes, but minimal — aim for images and one-line claims, not bullet walls
- **Medium**: in-person stage, speaking from notes (not teleprompter)
- **Opening**: needs a strong hook — this audience has watched plenty of mediocre keynotes

## Tone

> Conversational, concrete, honest. Willing to say "I used to think X, I was wrong, here's why." Not a TED talk (too polished). Not a status update (too dry). Think: sharp colleague sharing what they learned, not performing.

## What NOT to do

- Don't summarize the whole book — pick three things that matter and go deep
- Don't lecture — lead with examples, extract the pattern
- Don't use "System 1 / System 2" terminology cold — if needed, introduce them through story first
- Don't end with "any questions?" — end with a commitment ask
