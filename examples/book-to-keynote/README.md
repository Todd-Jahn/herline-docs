# Example: Book → Keynote

This example demonstrates turning a single book (plus a short user bio) into a 30-minute keynote speech — a use case that typically takes a subject-matter expert 2–4 weeks of drafting, redrafting, and rehearsal prep.

## What's demonstrated

- **End-to-end pipeline**: Library (D2B) → Studio → Courses (B2C) → Prep
- **Audience adaptation**: the same book, delivered differently for different audiences
- **Deliverable export**: keynote with timing annotations, ready to speak from

## The case

**Book**: *Thinking, Fast and Slow* by Daniel Kahneman (2011)
**User**: product manager with 8 years in consumer tech, speaking at a company all-hands
**Goal**: a 30-minute talk that helps product teams recognize and design around cognitive biases
**Title**: *"Why Your Users Don't Think What You Think They Do: Cognitive Biases in Product Decisions"*

## Files

- [`input.md`](input.md) — What was provided to Herline
- [`output-keynote.md`](output-keynote.md) — The 30-min keynote, ready to deliver
- [`generation-notes.md`](generation-notes.md) — Timing, process, human edits applied

## Why this case

*Thinking, Fast and Slow* is a dense, influential book most product thinkers have encountered but few can coherently present from. It's a good test case because:

1. **High knowledge density** — lots to cut, force real prioritization
2. **Abstract concepts** — need grounded examples, not just summaries
3. **Widely read** — outputs can be judged against a familiar baseline

The keynote doesn't try to cover the whole book. It picks 3 core ideas, grounds each in a product example, and gives the audience something to act on.
